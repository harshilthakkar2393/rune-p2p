---
title: Why no accounts
description: Every notes app I used broke the same way. Rune is the fix for that one thing.
---

Rune exists because the author kept losing the habit of writing, and each time the reason was the tool.

**Google Keep** for years. Then a note hit the character limit, there was no formatting, and every thought was readable by Google.

**Notion** for a while. The editor was exactly right. Then the block limit, the feature creep, and the fact that no encryption stands between an employee and your journal.

**Apple Notes.** Privacy is fixable there, but the control isn't yours.

**Plain files on the Mac.** The right instinct, and no phone. Writing stopped.

**A native local app** with cloud sync. Bad on the iPad, uncertain about where the sync went, and it liked to pop up on its own.

The pattern: every tool that could read your notes eventually gave you a reason to mind. Every tool that couldn't, couldn't reach your phone.

## The decision

Remove the party that can read. Not "encrypt so they can't", which still leaves an account, a password reset, a company. Remove the server. Then the only thing that syncs is the thing that holds the notes: your own devices, talking to each other.

Everything unusual about Rune is a consequence of that one removal. No login, because there's nothing to log in to. Twelve words, because a room needs a name and there's no one to hand out usernames. Sync only when both are open, because there's no one awake in between.

## Tool, not agent

The other half of the taste: the app waits to be opened. It never shows a notification, never scans your clipboard, never suggests what to write. It is a notebook. Notebooks don't have opinions.
